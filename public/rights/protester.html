A Protester in America

The right to gather and object to your government is not one clause but several, and it starts with the very first words of the Bill of Rights.

Descriptive, not legal advice · the founding documents' own words

Amendment I

Freedom of speech, of the press, to assemble peaceably, and to petition the government for a redress of grievances. The core of the right to protest, all in one sentence.

Amendment IV

Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures, which governs when police may stop, search, or detain you at a demonstration.

Amendment XIV

Due process and equal protection, which extend the First Amendment's limits to state and local governments, not just Congress.

The honest caveatTime, place, and manner rules are constitutional if they are content-neutral and leave open other channels. This page describes constitutional text, not the specific permit or public-order laws of any city.
